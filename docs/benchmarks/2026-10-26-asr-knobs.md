# ASR knobs: batch size and chunk length (2026-10-26)

Question: the pipeline spends about half of its wall time in the ASR. The GPU is
busy at ~94% but only ~7.5 GB of 15.9 GB of VRAM are used, and the engine is fed
20-second chunks with a batch of 2. Could a bigger batch, or fewer and longer
chunks, make the same work faster without touching the model?

Answer: **no.** The configuration already in use is the best of the four
measured, and longer chunks are dramatically worse.

## Method

- One fixed clip of 10 minutes, cut from an episode already processed
  (`ffmpeg -ss ... -t 600 -c copy`, no re-encode).
- The real pipeline (`python -m core.pipeline_cli`), same engine and models as
  production, one variant per run, each with its own `TP_DATA_DIR` so the
  working configuration is never modified.
- Phase timings read from the structured events (`@@TPEV@@`, `utils/events.py`);
  the demo punctuation step is off, so the numbers contain no CPU work.
- Harness: `bench/asr_ab.py` with `bench/asr_ab_variants.json`.
- `transcription_profile_locked` is forced to `false`: with its default (`true`)
  `Config.get_transcription_profile()` returns `maximum` no matter what the
  variant says, and the first attempt measured the same configuration four times
  before the batch line in the log made it visible.

## Results

| Variant | Profile | Batch | Chunk target | Wall | Demucs | **ASR** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `max-b2` (current) | maximum | 2 | 20 s | 115.8 s | 43.5 s | **64.3 s** |
| `bal-b8` | balanced | 8 | 20 s | 115.6 s | 43.5 s | **63.6 s** |
| `bat-b8` | batch | 8 | 20 s | 117.2 s | 43.6 s | **65.1 s** |
| `max-chunk45` | maximum | 2 | 45 s | 273.5 s | 43.4 s | **221.5 s** |

Reference machine: RX 9060 XT 16 GB, ROCm 7.0, torch 2.10.0+rocm7.0,
Qwen3-ASR-1.7B in bfloat16.

## What it means

- **Batch size does not matter** (64.3 -> 63.6 s, i.e. run-to-run noise). The
  work per chunk is fixed; batching changes parallelism, not the amount of
  computation. The free VRAM is not a lever for this engine.
- **Longer chunks are 3.4x slower** (64 -> 221 s with 11 chunks of 45 s instead
  of 26 of 20 s). Generation is autoregressive: more audio per chunk means more
  tokens to generate and a costlier attention pass, superlinearly. The 20 s
  target is not a conservative guess, it is the measured optimum.
- The split on this clip: Demucs 38%, ASR 55%, everything else ~2%.

## Consequences

- No configuration change follows from this measurement. `prova` on a full
  episode gave 25 and 27 minutes for 134 and 144 minutes of audio (~5.3x
  realtime), consistent with the per-phase split here.
- Remaining levers are structural, not configurational: overlapping Demucs and
  ASR (the pipeline is sequential by construction) or a different/lighter model
  (a quality decision that needs a human reference set, which does not exist
  yet).
- The output SRT differs slightly between runs of the same variant (553, 554 and
  556 cues across three runs): bfloat16 plus batched generation is not
  bit-exact. Any future A/B should therefore treat a few-cue difference as
  noise, not as a quality change.
